NEGATIVE RESULT: bidirectional relation gate hurts language modelling

Controlled comparison from the same base checkpoint with identical seed, LR
schedule, data order and step count. The ONLY difference is whether the relation
gate is active.

  base (start)                  val_loss 3.0111   ppl 20.3
  A. gate FROZEN at gamma=0     val_loss 2.9668   ppl 19.4   <- best
  B. gate TRAINABLE             val_loss 2.9863   ppl 19.8

  A vs B: -0.0194 (gate ON is WORSE)

The gap is stable across all five evaluation points
(+0.0155, +0.0147, +0.0174, +0.0171, +0.0174, +0.0195), so it is not noise.

METHODOLOGICAL CORRECTION (recorded because it matters):
I first reported 3.0111 -> 2.9863 as an improvement over the baseline. That was
wrong. Without a control run I could not distinguish the effect of the gate from
the effect of 6000 additional training steps. The entire apparent gain came from
the extra steps.

Root causes, all measured:

1. Gate signal far too weak. Deviation scale = |gamma| * std(s) ~= 0.006 against
   attention logit std 0.33 -- a 1.8 percent perturbation. The gate barely does
   anything while adding 4.1M trainable parameters, i.e. a noise source.

2. Relation vectors collapse. 32k tokens in r=16 dimensions: all pairwise
   cosines crowd into the 0.88-0.92 band. The top-scoring relations are
   meaningless (grape <-> measure, "re" <-> "ce").

3. No co-occurrence structure was learned. corr(PMI, relation score) = -0.0021
   over 1.56M token pairs. The model chose not to use the mechanism.

What still holds and is worth keeping:

- The gate implementation is CORRECT: gamma=0 is bit-exact identity, symmetry
  holds, KV-cache equivalence is 4e-7, and gamma does escape zero under training.
- code-as-index is viable: scripts/17_code_indexed_relation.py shows that
  code-derived u recovers the geometry with NO training (corr -0.6969, 98.4
  percent cluster agreement against a 6.2 percent random baseline).
  What failed is THIS parameterisation, not the design.
